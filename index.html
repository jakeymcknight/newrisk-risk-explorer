Which county are you planning for?

Select your county to see how its health facilities are exposed to extreme weather, where they are most likely to fail, and who depends on them. Assessments run on a 6–12 month cycle, timed to land before annual workplans are drafted.

⚠ Demonstration data. The facility register, MFL codes, KEPH levels, GPS positions, climate, flood, poverty and population data are all real. The assessment answers — every readiness score, staffing figure and utility reading — are synthetic, generated to exercise the tool ahead of fieldwork.

    Who is at risk, and which facilities carry them?

    The unit here is the populated place, not the facility. Every settled cell in Kilifi is scored for poverty and hazard, then attached to the facility nearest to it. Pick a question to start.

    Poorest to least poor

    five equal population groups

    The county's population split into five equal groups by relative wealth, poorest first. If the bar climbs from left to right, the burden falls on the poorest.

    Where those people are

    Each square is a populated cell, shaded by how exposed it is on the question above. Every mapped facility is drawn: a filled circle carries people who are both exposed and poor, sized by how many; a small hollow ring is a facility that carries none on this measure. A ring is a facility, not a gap.

    The hospitals that carry the county

    KEPH Level 4

    A dispensary cannot take a caesarean or a severe-malaria admission. Allocating every populated cell to its nearest Level 4 facility shows who depends on each hospital for care a dispensary cannot give.

    How long it actually takes to reach care

    published, modelled

    Straight-line distance ignores roads, rivers and terrain. These are published travel-time figures from a national model, the best available measure of real access, though only at sub-county resolution.
